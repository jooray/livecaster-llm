local transcription, remote reasoning

Livecaster keeps your episode plan as a live map while you record. Read down this page and it behaves the way your outline does during a session: what is behind you gets struck through, and what matters right now is the one thing in red.

A

What goes wrong halfway throughcovered

You write an outline for every interview. Forty minutes in you have lost track of what you already covered, and you find out weeks later in the edit — either you asked something that was already answered, or you forgot the one question you actually cared about.

Paper notes do not update themselves, and a second screen full of text is worse than nothing when you are the one talking.

B

The outline is the whole interfacecovered

Whatever your plan already looks like — headings, bullets, nested bullets, question lines, whole paragraphs — becomes a map. Covered lines take a single cut with the time beside them. A section you have finished folds to one line, in place, so what is left is what you see.

Nothing autoscrolls, nothing advances on your behalf, and nothing reorders your document. Bold a line and it stays marked until you have answered it.

C

Something three sections away just became reachable

“You mentioned you record on a headset — that is actually the thing that breaks this…”

Your guest wanders. A topic you had not reached yet lights up with the reason it lit up and a sentence you could use to get there from wherever the conversation actually is. You take it or you ignore it; the app never decides.

D

What you get when you press Finish

A slower model reads the whole transcript and writes the notes in the language of the podcast: a summary, what was covered and what was not, chapters with timestamps, titles and descriptions to choose from, a social post, quotes, and mentions with links — or an explicit link TODO where it could not find one, because it says so instead of inventing a URL.

Click any block to copy it, without its heading, ready to paste.

E

Privacy, and what it costs

Audio never leaves the machine. Transcription runs locally and the WAV backups are written beside the session. What crosses the network is the outline text, the transcript text, and the state derived from them.

Before you try it

  • Parakeet detects the language itself and cannot be told which one to use. You can lock it in the UI, or switch to Whisper, which honours it at ten times the latency.
  • Opening a Bluetooth headset's microphone drops the whole link to 16 kHz mono. That is macOS, not Livecaster.
  • With one shared microphone there are no speaker labels, and the app does not pretend otherwise.
  • On macOS the system-audio permission goes to your terminal app, and some terminals never raise the prompt. BlackHole is the documented fallback.
